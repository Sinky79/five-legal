Privacy Policy

Last updated 9 October 2026

Your record and everything you type stay on your phone. From version 1.3, Five sends anonymous usage counts so it can be improved, never anything that identifies you. Every claim below was checked against the source, not written from intention.

What the app stores

Everything you do in Five is written to a single file on your iPhone: when you started a session, what you typed as your first move, the dread ratings you chose, and any if-then plans you made. Nothing else on your phone can read it, and it is covered by your device’s own encryption.

What leaves your device

Your record never does, unless you send it yourself: Settings → Export data produces a file and hands it to you; where it goes after that is your choice. The only other thing that leaves is the anonymous usage counts below, which you can switch off.

Anonymous usage counts (from version 1.3)

Five sends a short note when certain things happen in the app, so its maker can see where it helps and where people get stuck: an onboarding step reached, the subscription screen shown, a trial or purchase, a five-minute session started, finished or stopped early, a plan created, the feedback link opened.

Each note carries only the event, a number or a fixed choice (which onboarding step, how many minutes, monthly or yearly), the app version, the app’s language, and a random ID made on your phone the first time a note is sent. It never carries what you type, your plans, your dread ratings, your name, your Apple ID, your device’s identifiers or your location. The notes go only to a server run by Five’s maker, which does not store your IP address.

To stop it: Settings → Share anonymous usage counts. Switching it off also discards anything not yet sent. Delete all data replaces the random ID, so nothing sent afterwards can be linked to what was sent before.

Purchases

Subscriptions are handled entirely by Apple. Five never sees your name, your card, or your Apple ID. It asks iOS one question — whether a subscription is currently active — and gets back yes or no.

From version 1.3, Five also shares purchase records (which plan, price, dates and country, under an anonymous ID) with RevenueCat, a subscription analytics service, so its maker can see trials and renewals. RevenueCat never receives what you type or anything that identifies you. Switching off Share anonymous usage counts stops this too.

Notifications

If you set a reminder for a plan, iOS schedules it on your device. No server is involved and the text of your reminder never leaves your phone.

What the app does not do

  • No advertising or tracking analytics; usage counts go only to Five’s own server, and purchase records only to RevenueCat (above)
  • No crash reporting
  • No advertising identifiers, no tracking, no profiling
  • No accounts, no sign-in, no email address collected
  • One third-party SDK, RevenueCat, for purchase records only (from version 1.3)

Deleting your data

Settings → Delete all data erases the file and any usage counts not yet sent. Deleting the app does the same. Counts already sent stay on Five’s server under a random ID that nothing links to you.

Children

Five is not directed at children, and collects nothing that identifies anyone of any age.

Changes

If this ever stops being true — if a future version syncs between devices, say — this page will change before that version ships, not after.
